📘 Section 1: 【Field Essay】Karaoke Amid the Roar of the Dam

Standing by the banks of the Dajia River, the massive concrete gates and steel structures of Shigang Dam tower with formidable presence, as rushing waters pour through the spillways, kicking up white foam and generating a deep, thunderous roar. Framed by lush green mountains in the distance, this massive engineering site—once the lifeline of regional water control and heavily damaged in the 1999 earthquake—exudes an austere and majestic authority.

Yet, walking along the path beside the dam into the shaded grove, that solemn engineering atmosphere instantly dissolves into the warmth of vibrant daily life.

A dense tree canopy spreads a blanket of cool green shade, where folding camping chairs, thermos flasks brought from home, and snack boxes are laid out neatly across stone benches. A group of local seniors sits gathered around a mobile karaoke machine, singing warmly alongside nostalgic backing tracks. Singing, laughter, and the rushing water from the nearby dam interweave into a wonderfully multi-layered soundscape.

What was once a heavily guarded hydraulic facility has been gently reclaimed by community life. People gather here to brew tea, chat, walk dogs, and socialize, treating this colossal infrastructure as their very own backyard. The cool mist from the splashing water drifts into the shade, blending with the echo of karaoke microphones to reveal the resilient and welcoming lifestyle of rural Taiwan.

📖 Section 3: 【Flash Fiction】Chorus in the Backyard

The water cascading from the dam gates sounded like an endless rumble of distant thunder.

Uncle Lin, in his early sixties, parked his wheeled karaoke speaker beneath a dark pine tree and plugged in the microphone. On the stone bench sat thermos flasks filled with hot water brought from home and a canister of Oolong tea leaves. His son unfolded the lawn chairs while his ten-year-old grandson leaned against the walkway railing, clutching a cookie while staring at the rushing foam in the distance.

"Grandpa, can't we walk up there onto the top of the dam gate to look?" the boy asked, pointing toward the colossal structure.

"Dream on! It's completely locked up now. No way they'd let you up there," the son laughed, playfully patting the boy's head.

Uncle Lin poured a cup of hot tea, eyes twinkling with nostalgia. "When I was your age, this dam had only been built for a few years! There were no security gates back then. Whenever a typhoon passed and the floodwaters rose, us kids didn't know fear—we'd all charge right onto the dam top! Standing up there watching monstrous waves crash below, with the ground shaking under our feet, we shouted over the roar of the water..."

"Yeah, Dad watched waves when he was a kid, but when I was a kid, I was dodging static shocks," the son gestured to the paved walkway, grinning at the boy. "After the 921 earthquake fractured the dam, the government rebuilt this trail with plastic-wood handrails... In dry winters, touching them meant a sharp static shock! For years, village rumors warned that 'the dam trail shocks people,' so my generation walked with hands tucked tightly in our pockets!"

Uncle Lin burst into laughter. "Everyone was terrified of static shocks, but everyone still wanted to come see if the dam was fixed!"

Completed in 1977, the dam had seen Uncle Lin's generation as fearless boys watching waves, his son's generation dodging static-shock handrails, and now his grandson listening to stories under the shade. Decades of time, bound gently together by hot tea brought from home and a few old songs in this community backyard of Shigang.
